1. Project starting place: "Where things end up"
Lifted from the intro lecture: given something entering the water at a point in region R, where has the surface flow taken it a week, a month, a year later? 
The deliverable is a surface-transport operator (a transition matrix or equivalent) that another analyst could load and iterate forward.
Justification:
It naturally splits into three streams that build on each other rather than existing separately: 
    - a computational layer (build the gridded transition estimates from 16GB of ragged, unevenly-sampled data)
    - a statistical layer (the transition estimates are unreliable where drifters are sparse → inferential work, not just descriptive stats)
    - a business layer (what the resulting connectivity pattern means and to whom).
Genuine, non-trivial statistics problem baked in: drifters cluster where the flow converges and thin out where it diverges, so raw cell-by-cell transition counts are noisy and biased toward well-sampled regions. 
Smoothing/regularising this defensibly is exactly what MATH3871 (Bayesian Inference and Computation) and MATH3821 (Statistical Modelling and Computation) are for.
It gives the business student a bounded, well-defined analytical seat (translating connectivity + uncertainty into a real-world risk/economic narrative) rather than an open-ended one.
Not locked into this - projects are "guidelines towards a minimally viable product" and  may evolve from the starting point. But it's a strong fit for stream mix, so this plan is built around it.
Build in one extension from the start: the GDP overview document notes that "predicting where a drifter goes next".
Differs from "where things end up" only in scale; a single drifter's near-term path versus the whole field's transport pattern. 
Transition matrix implicitly makes point predictions, so comparing those against the persistence and mean-flow baselines is a coherent, low-cost add-on.
It gives a second, independent way to demonstrate main product actually works, rather than asking the reader to take the transition matrix on faith.

4. Task ownership by stream
Computational stream (2 people)
Build the data access + wrangling pipeline: lazily loading the Zarr store via clouddrift/xarray, since the full dataset (~16GB, 197M rows) won't fit in memory - this is squarely a COMP9313 (Big Data Management) problem (chunked/out-of-core processing).
Implement the spatial gridding and transition-matrix construction (binning drifter positions into cells, counting transitions between cells over chosen time lags) - COMP3121 (Algorithm Design) for making this efficient at scale, COMP2521/COMP2041 for a clean, testable, version-controlled codebase.
Represent the transition matrix as a directed graph where useful (which cells are reachable from which, absorbing "coastline" states) - a natural application of MATH1081 (Discrete Mathematics) graph theory, and a nice bridge to COMP9417 (Machine Learning) if  want a supplementary predictive baseline later.
Own COMP3311 (Database Systems) aspects of structuring intermediate outputs so the quantitative stream can query them without re-running the whole pipeline.

Quantitative stream (2 people)
Own the core statistical problem: raw cell-to-cell transition counts are unreliable wherever drifters are sparse, and sparsity itself is not random (drifters gather where flow converges). This needs regularisation, not a raw frequency table.
MATH3871 (Bayesian Inference and Computation): put a prior over transition probabilities and borrow strength across neighbouring cells (hierarchical/empirical Bayes shrinkage). 
Don't stop at applying it - quantify how much it changes the picture: report how far the naive (unregularised) transition estimates drift from the regularised ones in the sparsest cells, and run a sensitivity check on how much the choice of prior strength affects the conclusions. 
That comparison is what turns "we used a Bayesian method" into a demonstrated result.
MATH2801 (Theory of Statistics): formal treatment of the estimators and their properties.
MATH3821 (Statistical Modelling and Computation): bootstrap/resampling-based uncertainty quantification as a complement or check on the Bayesian approach.
MATH2501 (Linear Algebra): eigen-decompose the transition matrix for its stationary distribution and mixing time - where does material end up in the long run, are there near-absorbing "sink" cells, how fast does the system forget its starting point. This is a strong, visual result for the poster, not just a theoretical aside.
MATH2871 (Data Management for Statistical Analysis): the statistical-side data wrangling once the computational stream hands off gridded output.

Business stream (1 person)
"Why this matters" case for the region (feeds the README and report background), grounded in literature as the past-student document recommends.
Own one clearly scoped analytical contribution: apply ECON2209 (Business Forecasting) techniques to the seasonal structure of the transition estimates - do connectivity patterns shift predictably by season? 
Treat this as a real forecasting exercise with a train/test split (e.g. forecast one year's seasonal pattern having only seen prior years), not a descriptive seasonal chart. 
Then quantify what a validated seasonal risk window is actually worth to a real decision-maker (e.g. spill-response planning, fisheries larvae dispersal, shipping risk) - a specific, defensible framing beats a general one. 
Lead the translation of the modelling output into the non-technical narrative for the report's executive summary, poster and presentation.
The past-student document is explicit that this needs to be written "for aliens," and that's a genuinely valuable, proportionate role for one person rather than asking them to match five people's technical output.
ECON2206/ECON3208/ECON3210 (introductory/applied/big-data econometrics) can inform how the forecasting and validation are framed and diagnosed, without duplicating the quantitative stream's inferential work.
Single-point-of-failure mitigation: the business stream owns the literature/significance case, the ECON2209 forecasting analysis, and the non-technical narrative - three deliverables on one person. 
Since the forecasting analysis is largely quantitative/computational in nature, the quantitative and computational streams will provide backup support on that specific task (train/test split design, sanity-checking the forecast against the transition-matrix output) to soften the load, without taking ownership of it away from the business stream.

7. Decision log (settled 2026-09-26 after lecturer feedback; evidence in outputs/results/adequacy_by_resolution.csv and data_quality.py output)
1. Cell shape - square lat/lon cells kept; hexagons not adopted. Hexagons (Uber's H3 system) have six equidistant neighbours and near-equal area on the sphere, but: transition probabilities are conditional on the start cell, so unequal cell areas don't bias them; the area effect here is modest (a 2 deg cell at 41S is 24% smaller than one at 5S) and is corrected by dividing by cell area wherever a result is read as a density; hexes add a dependency, polygon plotting and harder explanation for no change to the core method. Downstream code uses only flat cell ids, so a hex grid stays a one-function swap (Week 8 optional check).
2. Neighbour-informed adequacy - the observation is right: the Week 1 check judges each cell on its own drifters. That is deliberate, because it measures the naive estimate, and the share of cells failing it is the size of the problem shrinkage has to solve. Pooling neighbours inside the adequacy check would need a pooling weight, which is exactly what the Week 6 model estimates, and pooling blindly across a current front (e.g. the Agulhas retroflection) would mix unlike flows. Neighbour borrowing is therefore built into the model (2 deg rows shrink toward their 4 deg parent) and shown as a before/after adequacy map in Week 6.
3. Adequacy threshold - N=100 kept. Worst-case SE of a proportion is sqrt(0.25/n); SE <= 0.05 gives n >= 100 (95% interval about +/-0.10). The 95%-CI version, half-width <= 0.05, gives n >= 1.96^2 x 0.25 / 0.05^2 = 384.2, so 385. No 1-4 deg cell reaches 385 distinct drifters (1% at 5 deg), so that threshold marks every cell inadequate and carries no information about where the data can be trusted. Both are precision targets rather than tests; SE <= 0.05 is stated as ours.
4. Runtime - why the original plan subsampled: streaming the ~16GB cloud store took ~10 minutes per run, too slow to develop against. load_region() now extracts R once (~9 min) and caches it, so every script runs on all 19.3M readings in under 2 minutes, and the Week 3 transition table is smaller again (~1 row per drifter-day). The subsample and the "full data later" week are dropped. Remaining runtime risks are the Week 7 bootstrap (hundreds of refits) and the Week 8 region swap (one new ~9 min extract per region).
5. Coarse baseline - adopted, at 4 deg. The lecturer's suggestion: build the whole chain (transition matrix, long-run maps, narrative) first on large cells where almost every cell has enough data, so the project has a complete, defensible answer early, then refine. 4 deg because 80% of ocean cells are adequate (3 deg: 67%, 5 deg: 81%, so coarsening further buys almost nothing), and 4 deg cells nest exactly in the 2 deg and 1 deg grids (3 deg does not), which lets the baseline serve as the shrinkage prior for 2 deg (decision 2). Limitation stated up front: 4 deg cells (~440 km) are too coarse to separate the coastal strip from open ocean, which is the reason to go to 2 deg. The 4 deg grid overhangs the box's northern edge by half a row.
6. Drogue - all drifters are the main estimate; drogue status is kept per transition. Drogued-only data leaves 0.3% of 2 deg cells and 9% of 4 deg cells adequate, so it cannot support a transition matrix at any useful resolution. 68% of readings are undrogued, and undrogued drifters feel the wind directly, which arguably suits floating material like oil and debris (Business to confirm from literature). The proposal states the product describes near-surface transport including direct wind effects; Week 8 compares drogued and undrogued transitions at 4 deg to size the difference.
7. Years - all years as the main estimate (recommended; team to confirm in Week 3). Adequacy needs every drifter, and 2010+ becomes a Week 8 sensitivity check on start_time, with no rebuild needed.
Decisions 8-11 were made on 2026-09-28 while building the Week 3 transition table (evidence from transitions.py output and checks logged in logs/ai_use_log.md).
8. Exit rule - a drifter "left R" if its own hourly record covers start + tau but has no reading inside R at that hour; end_date is not used. The original step 1 used end_date >= start + tau as "still transmitting". Checked before building: end_date (the DAC's "last good date") matched the drifter's last hourly reading within 1 hour for only 7% of R's 1,975 drifters and is missing for 131 of them, and 49% of R's drifters have gaps in their hourly record (8,751 gaps, median 20 h). On the real table the end_date rule would have labelled 18,626 rows as exits, 6,577 of which are gaps and 922 ended records, and missed 527 real exits (from drifters with no end_date): about 60% too many exits. Since exits are a key result (how much material leaves the Benguela box), the rule uses the record itself.
9. Where the per-drifter data lives - a separate module and cache (src/ingest/drifter_records.py, data/interim/<region>_records.npz) rather than adding it to load.py as step 1 originally said. The record stretches need every reading of each drifter, including those outside R, which the region cache doesn't keep. A separate cache means (a) everyone's existing region cache stays valid instead of forcing a ~10 min rebuild, (b) load.py keeps one job (readings inside R), and (c) scripts that loop over every array in the region cache (e.g. the missing-values check in data_quality.py) are unaffected. Building it takes under a minute.

Unknown destinations - rows whose drifter's record ended before start + tau, or had a gap at start + tau, are kept in the table with an outcome code (2 = ended, 3 = gap) instead of being dropped as step 2 originally said. They are excluded from counts, so P is unchanged, but their share (3.3% of starts) can be reported, and ended records with typedeath = 1 (ran aground) are exactly what a Week 6 coastline state would need.
