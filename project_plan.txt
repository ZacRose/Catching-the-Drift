Key terms:
- R: our region, the box in section 2.
- Drogue: a sea anchor hanging below the drifter so it moves with the water rather than the wind.
  Many drifters lose theirs ("undrogued").
- Cell: one square of the grid laid over R, e.g. 2 x 2 degrees.
- tau: the time lag. Transition tracks where the drifter is tau days later.
- Transition matrix P: an entry (row i, column j) gives the probability that a drifter starting in cell
  i will be in cell j (or has left R completely) after tau days. P^n (P multiplied by
  itself n times) forecasts n x tau days into future.
- Naive P: P straight from counts (count / row total). No adjustments (e.g. smoothing) or 
  statistical cleaning.
- Shrinkage: blending each small cell's own data (e.g. 2 degree, often thin) with the data of the
  larger cell containing it (e.g. 4 degree, with more drifters).
- Prior row / alpha: in our shrinkage model, a 2 deg cell's row starts from its 4 deg cell's row
  (the "prior"). alpha says how much that prior counts, in made-up transitions: a cell with alpha
  real transitions is half its own data, half its 4 deg cell.
- Train / validation / test drifters: we hide some drifters while fitting P, then check how well
  P predicts where they went. Validation drifters are used to pick settings such as alpha; test
  drifters are only used once, for the final score.
- Held-out log-likelihood: the score for that check. For each hidden transition, take the log of
  the probability P gave to where the drifter actually went, and average. Closer to 0 is better;
  a model that gave probability 0 to something that happened scores minus infinity.
- Persistence baseline: the simplest possible forecast, "material stays in the cell it started
  in". Any useful P should beat it.

1. The project: "Where things end up"

Question (from the intro lecture): if something goes into the water at a point in R, where has the
surface flow taken it a week, a month, a year later?
Deliverable: a transition matrix that another analyst can load and step forward in time, with its
uncertainty attached.

Why did we choose this project:
- It splits into three streams that work well together and build off each other: computational 
  (turn 16GB of hourly, unevenly spaced records into gridded transition counts i.e. a tally sheet of 
  drifter movements based on tau), quantitative (counts are unreliable where drifters are sparse,
  meaning we have a statistical inference problem) and business (what this connectivity pattern means for
  real world value, and for whom).
- Strong statistics problem. Drifters congregate where currents converge and thin out where they
  diverge, so raw counts are noisy and biased towards well-sampled areas. Statistical smoothing and other
  defensible techniques will draw heavily on MATH3871 (Bayesian Inference and Computation) and MATH3821
  (Statistical Modelling and Computation).
- Business impact is clearly defined (turning connectivity and uncertainty into a risk or economic story)
  rather than open-ended and vague.

Built-in extension: "predicting where a drifter goes next" (GDP overview document). Can use matrix P for
predictions and assess these against a baseline (e.g. predict where you are, or follow the average flow). 
This also validates that P works, rather than asking the reader to trust it blindly. 

Projects are "guidelines towards a minimally viable product", so this can change as we go.

Computational - DONE (2026-09-28, branch transMatrix, merged)
Run: python3 -m src.gridding.transitions (the first run also builds the per-drifter cache in
data/interim/).
1. Telling "left R" apart from "stopped reporting": src/ingest/drifter_records.py fetches each
   drifter's end_date, typedeath and the unbroken stretches of its hourly record (in or out of R),
   cached in data/interim/<region>_records.npz. Whether a drifter was still reporting is decided
   from these stretches, not end_date (decision 8). It is a separate module and cache, so load.py
   and the region cache are unchanged (decision 9).
2. build_transitions(): one row per drifter per day, starting at 00:00 UTC. (Hourly starts from
   the same drifter are near-copies and would inflate counts 24 times without adding
   information.) Each row has drifter_id, start_time, start and end lon/lat, drogue status at the
   start, typedeath and an outcome code:
       0  in R          an ordinary cell-to-cell move
       1  left R        still reporting at start + tau, but from outside R
       2  record ended  the record stopped before start + tau: destination unknown
       3  gap           start + tau falls in a gap in the record: destination unknown
   Codes 2 and 3 stay in the table but aren't counted (decision 10). Positions are stored instead
   of cell ids, so one table serves every grid size and any drogue or year filter without a
   rebuild. typedeath (1 = ran aground) is kept for a possible coastline state in Week 6.
3. transition_counts(): a (cells + 1) x (cells + 1) count matrix built with grid.cell_index, the
   extra row/column being "left R", plus distinct drifters per start cell. row_normalise() gives
   the naive P, with "left R" absorbing (decision 11).
4. Tests: tests/test_transitions.py on hand-made tracks - stay, move, exit, record ended, gap,
   re-entering R before tau, a target exactly on a record boundary, shuffled input - and rows of
   P sum to 1. 14 tests, all passing.
5. tau = 15 days by default (the 2 deg crossing time above). The same tau is used at every grid
   size, so grid comparisons aren't mixed up with a change of lag. At 4 deg most drifters are
   still in their cell after 15 days, which is fine; Week 7 will test the Markov assumption directly.
6. Outputs in data/processed/:
   - transitions_tau15d.npz: 803,368 drifter-day starts from 1,971 drifters (4 drifters visit R
     but are never there at 00:00 UTC). Outcomes: 95.2% in R, 1.5% left R, 2.4% record ended,
     0.9% gap.
   - transition_counts_2deg_tau15d.npz: 776,777 counted transitions on the 2 deg grid.
   - naive_P_4deg_tau15d.npz: 161 cells with transitions; the median chance of still being in
     the same 4 deg cell after 15 days is 0.53.
   Checks: a separate brute-force lookup on 300 random rows plus 50 of each rare outcome found no
   mismatches. "Left R" rows start a median 0.7 deg from the box edge (10.6 deg for the rest). The
   median 15-day displacement is 153 km (about 0.12 m/s), in line with data_quality.py. Rebuilt
   from live data on 2026-10-01: identical, array for array.

Quantitative - DONE (2026-10-02, branch week3-shrinkage; reviewed and merged into week3-unified)
Run, in this order: python3 -m src.inference.split, then src.inference.evaluate,
src.inference.transition_adequacy and src.inference.displacement_eda (each under 2 minutes).
All four read the Week 3 table through src/inference/transition_data.py, which keeps the rows
with a known destination ("stayed in R" and "left R": 776,777 of 803,368) and drops the rest.
1. Estimator chosen: shrinkage of each 2 deg row towards its 4 deg cell's row
   (src/inference/estimators.py; the maths is a Dirichlet-multinomial, MATH3871). For
   cell i:  p_ij = (n_ij + alpha x m_ij) / (n_i + alpha), where n are the cell's own counts and
   m is the 4 deg row spread over its four 2 deg cells. Few drifters -> close to the 4 deg row;
   many -> close to the cell's own counts. alpha was picked on the validation drifters:
   alpha = 56 (figure: alpha_sensitivity.png, which also shows the test score for every alpha
   as the prior-sensitivity check). The drifter-level bootstrap (the "do both" in the original
   step) is not done yet; it is the Week 7 uncertainty check.
2. Assessment designed and run before any final model (src/inference/split.py, evaluate.py).
   Drifters, not rows, are split, because one drifter's daily transitions overlap: 64% train
   (1,211 drifters), 16% validation (303), 20% test (378), fixed seed, saved in
   outputs/results/drifter_split.csv so every later model is scored on the same test drifters.
   Results (outputs/results/evaluation_tau15d.csv; higher = better, compare within a grid only):
       grid   model                    test score   test moves given probability 0
       4 deg  naive P                  -inf         0.06%   (-1.34 on the rest)
       4 deg  persistence (eps 0.5)    -3.14        0%
       2 deg  naive P                  -inf         0.76%   (-2.02 on the rest)
       2 deg  persistence (eps 0.7)    -5.36        0%
       2 deg  shrinkage to 4 deg       -2.07        0%
   What this says: naive P fails outright at 2 deg, because 0.76% of real test moves were never
   seen in training. Shrinkage never gives probability 0 and beats persistence by a wide margin.
   On the moves naive P can score, shrinkage is very slightly worse (-2.024 vs -2.016), so its
   gain so far comes entirely from the rare unseen moves. Read Week 4 open issues g-i before
   quoting this as "shrinkage is better".
3. Adequacy redone on the transitions (src/inference/transition_adequacy.py,
   outputs/results/transition_adequacy_tau15d.csv, transition_adequacy_map.png). Counting
   distinct drifters that start a 15-day move in each cell, instead of any reading:
       2 deg: 41.3% of 598 ocean cells adequate (Week 1 check: 46.0%); median 90 drifters
       4 deg: 78.4% of 162 ocean cells adequate (Week 1 check: 80.2%); median 155 drifters
   These are the numbers for the proposal. The counts match the start_drifters arrays saved by
   Computational exactly. A drifter starts about 15 moves per 2 deg cell (30 per 4 deg cell),
   which is why drifters, not moves, are counted.
4. Association with the response (src/inference/displacement_eda.py,

       before 2010 160 km vs 2010+ 149 km      (decision 7: small)
   Caution: the groups also differ by where drifters were, so these are first looks, not
   controlled comparisons. Drogue status is recorded at the start of each move only (issue e).

7. Decision log

Decisions 1-7 were settled on 2026-09-26 after lecturer feedback (evidence:
outputs/results/adequacy_by_resolution.csv and the data_quality.py output). Decisions 8-11 were
made on 2026-09-28 while building the Week 3 table (evidence: transitions.py output and the checks
in logs/ai_use_log.md).

1. Cell shape: square lat/lon cells, not hexagons.
   Hexagons (Uber's H3 system) have six equally spaced neighbours and near-equal areas. We don't
   need them: transition probabilities are conditional on the start cell, so unequal areas don't
   bias them; the area difference is modest (a 2 deg cell at 41S is 24% smaller than one at 5S)
   and is corrected by dividing by cell area whenever a result is read as a density; and hexagons
   add a dependency, harder plotting and harder explanation for no change to the method.
   Downstream code uses only flat cell ids, so hexagons stay a one-function swap (optional Week 8
   check).

2. Adequacy judges each cell on its own drifters, not its neighbours'.
   This is deliberate. It measures the naive estimate, and the share of cells that fail is the
   size of the problem shrinkage has to solve. Pooling neighbours inside the check would need a
   pooling weight, which is exactly what the Week 6 model estimates, and pooling blindly across a
   current front (e.g. the Agulhas retroflection) would mix unlike flows. Borrowing from
   neighbours is built into the model instead (2 deg rows shrink towards their 4 deg parent) and
   shown as a before/after adequacy map in Week 6.

3. Adequacy threshold: N = 100 distinct drifters.
   The worst-case standard error of a proportion is sqrt(0.25/n); SE <= 0.05 gives n >= 100 (a 95%
   interval of about +/-0.10). The stricter version, a 95% interval of +/-0.05, needs
   n >= 1.96^2 x 0.25 / 0.05^2 = 384.2, so 385. No cell at 1-4 deg reaches 385 (1% do at 5 deg),
   so that threshold fails every cell and says nothing about where the data can be trusted. Both
   are precision targets we chose, not tests, and the proposal says SE <= 0.05 is ours.

4. Runtime: no subsampling.
   The original plan subsampled because streaming the ~16GB cloud store took about 10 minutes per
   run. load_region() now extracts R once (~9 min) and caches it, so every script runs on all
   19.3M readings in under 2 minutes, and the transition table is smaller again (about one row
   per drifter-day). The subsample and the "full data later" week are dropped. Remaining runtime
   risk: the Week 8 region swap (a new ~9 min extract per region). The bootstrap turned out cheap:
   1000 refits of the 2 deg P take about 1.5 minutes (Week 6, 2026-10-29). Building the table at
   six lags with its checks takes 2-3 minutes (Week 7, 2026-10-29). WE NEED TO DOUBLE CHECK IF THESE NUMBERS (e.g. 10mins, 2mins) ARE STILL TRUE
   BEFORE USING IN README

5. Coarse baseline at 4 deg.
   Lachlan's suggestion: build the whole chain (P, long-run maps, story) first on large cells
   where nearly every cell has enough data, so we have a complete, defensible answer early, then
   refine. 4 deg because 80% of ocean cells are adequate (3 deg: 67%, 5 deg: 81%, so going
   coarser buys almost nothing), and 4 deg cells nest exactly inside the 2 deg and 1 deg grids
   (3 deg cells don't), so the baseline can act as the shrinkage prior for 2 deg (decision 2).
   Stated limitation: 4 deg cells (~440 km) are too coarse to separate the coastal strip from
   the open ocean, which is why we move to 2 deg. The 4 deg grid overhangs the box's northern
   edge by half a row.

6. Drogue: use all drifters, and keep drogue status on every transition.
   Drogued-only data leaves 0.3% of 2 deg cells and 9% of 4 deg cells adequate, so it can't
   support P at any useful resolution. 68% of readings are undrogued. Undrogued drifters feel the
   wind directly, which arguably suits floating material like oil and debris (Business to confirm
   from the literature). The proposal will say P describes near-surface transport including
   direct wind effects; Week 8 compares drogued and undrogued transitions at 4 deg to size the
   difference.

6a. Sensitivity check: all drifters vs undrogued drifters only (Stella, 2026-09-28; reworded
   2026-10-03 after checking the sources).
   Undrogued-only data is thin but usable: 0.7% / 22.4% / 49.5% / 57.4% of ocean cells are
   adequate at 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 deg (data_quality.py), against 0.0% / 0.3% / 1.8% / 9.3% for
   drogued-only. So undrogued-only can be compared with the main estimate at 4 deg, and drogued-only
   can't.
   Why it matters: undrogued drifters respond more strongly to wind and to the fast top few
   centimetres of the ocean, and in the Gulf of Mexico they were found to spread and come ashore
   much like the Deepwater Horizon oil (Novelli et al. 2020; Grodsky et al. 2011; Laxague et al.
   2018). Published drifter debris and transition-matrix studies pool both kinds (van Sebille et
   al. 2012; McAdam & van Sebille 2018) or check them separately (Maximenko et al. 2012), which
   supports decision 6 as the main estimate.
   Caution: this is a handful of studies, not a settled result, and the Gulf of Mexico drifters
   may not be the same buoy type as ours. Most of these papers were checked from abstracts only
   by Liam and AI Lit Review agent.

7. Years: all years for the main estimate.
   Adequacy needs every drifter. 2010+ becomes a Week 8 sensitivity check, a filter on
   start_time with no rebuild.

8. Exit rule: a drifter "left R" if its own hourly record covers start + tau but it has no
   reading inside R at that hour. end_date is not used.
   The original step used end_date >= start + tau to mean "still transmitting". We checked first:
   end_date (the data centre's "last good date") matched the drifter's last hourly reading within
   1 hour for only 7% of R's 1,975 drifters and is missing for 131 of them, and 49% of R's
   drifters have gaps in their hourly record (8,751 gaps, median 20 h). On the real table the
   end_date rule would have labelled 18,626 rows as exits - 6,577 of them actually gaps and 922
   ended records - and missed 527 real exits from drifters with no end_date: about 60% too many
   exits. Exits are a key result (how much material leaves the Benguela box), so the rule uses
   the record itself.

9. Per-drifter data has its own module and cache (src/ingest/drifter_records.py,
   data/interim/<region>_records.npz) instead of going into load.py.
   The record stretches need every reading of each drifter, including those outside R, which the
   region cache doesn't keep. A separate cache means (a) everyone's region cache stays valid (no
   ~10 min rebuild), (b) load.py keeps one job, and (c) scripts that loop over every array in the
   region cache (e.g. the missing-values check in data_quality.py) are unaffected. Building it
   takes under a minute. ONCE AGAIN, DOUBLE CHECK THIS

10. Unknown destinations are kept, not dropped.
   Rows whose record ended, or had a gap, at start + tau stay in the table with outcome 2 or 3.
   They aren't counted, so P is unchanged, but their share (3.3% of starts) can be reported, and
   ended records with typedeath = 1 (ran aground) are what a Week 6 coastline state needs.
